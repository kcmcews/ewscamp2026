Theme Talk 1

Shining As Stars (1): Holy Father

Phil 2:15; Lev 19:2; Mat 5:48 (ESV)

Sermon Outlines

“...that you may be blameless and innocent, children of God without blemish in the midst of a crooked and twisted generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world...”

— Philippians 2:15 (ESV)
1. Introduction
  • Theme: Holiness or Holy Living
  • "Shining as Stars" helps us understand what holy living means. How did the Early Church shine as stars in the Roman world?
    • Core Moral Differences: Sanctity of Life (banned abortion, child abandonment, gladiator games); Sexual Purity (virginity until marriage, faithfulness); Charity & Care (fed poor, cared for plague victims, buried dead); Peaceful Living.
    • Separation from Roman Culture: No Idol Worship; Honest Work (avoided jobs tied to idols, theatre, magic); Close Community (private homes, shared meals).
  • Holy living is NOT:
    • Just being religious (One can be religious and yet not holy; cf. Amos 5:21-24).
    • Being moral in a negative or legalistic sense (Not life-denying or Pharisaic).
    • Confused with middle-class respectability / snobbery.
    • Confused with the ability to do signs, wonders, or miracles (cf. Mat 7:21-23; Samson).
2. In What Sense is God Holy?
  • Root meaning is 'to separate':
    • Applied to God: Separate from creation (infinite from finite, perfect from imperfect). God's moral perfection in contrast to our imperfection.
    • Applied to humanity: Separated from the world and unto God (sanctified).
  • A Trinitarian Definition for Holiness:
    • Father (Mt 5:48): We are called to become more like our Father ("be like Daddy").
    • Son (1 Cor 11:1): Jesus is the true Son; we model our lives after Him.
    • Spirit (Gal 5:19-23): Filled with the Spirit to display the fruit of the Spirit.
  • The Incomparable God (Isaiah 40:12-26):
    • Is there anyone wise and strong enough to teach God? (40:12-14)
    • Kingdoms rise and fall, but God remains Lord of history. (40:15-17)
    • The gods of this world are not comparable to Him. (40:18-20)
    • Kings come and go, but Jesus Christ is the same forever. (40:21-24)
    • If you are still worried, look at the stars. (40:25-26)
3. The Holy God Who Does Not Tolerate Sin
  • Scripture Context: Exodus 32:7-10; 33:12-23
  • God's wrath against a stiff-necked people (Ex 32:9-10).
  • Our God is a consuming fire; let us offer acceptable worship with reverence and awe (Heb 12:28b-29).
  • Moses' prayer: "Please show me your glory" (Ex 33:18-23).

Group Discussion Questions

Question 1

Is there a danger that often the gospel of justification by faith and acceptance by God has been presented as "cheap grace" and Christians have treated God more like an indulgent godfather than a holy Father? How serious is that problem in your church and in your life? Examples?

Question 2

How should we interpret passages like Matthew 5:29-30? Should it be interpreted literally? Alternatively, should we ignore passages like this?

Question 3

Do you dare to pray Moses' prayer, "Please show me your glory"? Should we pray it?
